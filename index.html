The case, month by month

  • Dx
    64-year-old woman — diagnosis
    EBV-positive colorectal diffuse large B-cell lymphoma

    Diagnosed after weight loss, abdominal pain, and anemia: Epstein-Barr virus–positive of the rectosigmoid colon with bowel, mesenteric, and splenic involvement, complicated by contained perforation and a drained pelvic abscess — a heavy inflammatory burden before therapy even began.

    Background history: Graves disease treated by remote thyroidectomy (on levothyroxine), and premature ventricular complexes noted before chemotherapy.

  • 0
    Before cycle 1 — the pivotal result
    Screening MUGA: LVEF 43.1%, mildly enlarged LV

    A screening showed LVEF 43.1% with a mildly enlarged left ventricle and no regional wall-motion abnormality — before any anthracycline.

    A baseline LVEF below 50% is the high-risk category, for which the 2022 ESC guideline recommends cardiology referral before treatment, cardioprotective therapy, and imaging before, during, and after therapy.

    No pre-treatment strain imaging or cardiac biomarkers were obtained.

  • 1–6
    Treatment
    R-CHOP → R-CEOP after cycle 1; six cycles; metabolic response

    was started. An echocardiogram after cycle 1 read LVEF ≈45%, and etoposide replaced doxorubicin () "due to her lower EF." Doxorubicin exposure was reportedly limited to cycle 1 — roughly 50 mg/m² at standard dosing (outside records pending).

    Read carefully, the 43.1% → 45% "change" crosses two different modalities and sits far inside . Dysfunction was recognized after cycle 1, not caused by it.

    Six cycles were completed with complete metabolic response on PET ().

  • 7–18
    The second hit builds
    Fistula takedown, recurrent abscesses, malnutrition, DVT

    A chronic enterocutaneous fistula developed and required takedown with small-bowel resection at month 17. The postoperative course brought recurrent intra-abdominal collections, a drained anterior mediastinal collection, malnutrition, severe deconditioning, and a catheter-associated right upper-extremity DVT.

    Through all of it — 17 months — no one measured the ventricle.

  • 19
    Heart-failure admission
    LVEF <10% · NT-proBNP 25,535 · clean coronaries

    Five days of exertional dyspnea, orthopnea, and edema; sinus tachycardia and elevated JVP without hypoperfusion.

  • 22
    Recovery
    LVEF 38–40% — her pre-treatment range — in 3 months

    NT-proBNP fell >95% (25,535 → 1,337 → 659, plateauing near 1,000 pg/mL). Echo at 3 months: LVEF 38% biplane (40% visual), GLS −13%, normal cavity size, no mitral regurgitation, normal RV. The defibrillator vest was discontinued.

    By 5 months she was NYHA II, euvolemic, and had regained 16 kg. She meets the definition of — which is a reason to continue therapy, not to stop it ().

    Still owed: IV iron (TSAT 12%), genetic testing, and reconciliation of a month-21 surveillance PET (Deauville 5 peritoneal nodules, unconfirmed).

Why CMR settles the differential: no subendocardial, coronary-territory scar (and calcium score 0) → not ischemic. No T2 edema → not active myocarditis. Anthracycline injury usually leaves no LGE at all. A thin mid-wall septal stripe is replacement fibrosis — gadolinium pooling in collagen-expanded interstitium — the hallmark of non-ischemic dilated cardiomyopathy, often genetic, and a marker of sudden-death risk and incomplete recovery. It is the fixed substrate beneath a reversible collapse.

Three hits, one collapse

Pre-existing substrate

LVEF 43.1% and an enlarged LV before any therapy. A mildly dilated, hypokinetic ventricle at 63 is how many genetic cardiomyopathies first declare themselves — are 7× enriched in "cancer-therapy cardiomyopathy." Genetic testing is pending.

Limited anthracycline

One reported cycle, ~50 mg/m². In a 2,625-patient cohort, 98% of anthracycline cardiotoxicity appeared within the first year (median 3.5 months); her collapse came at 13 months. Possible contributor — unproven cause.

Inflammatory–catabolic stress

Surgery, recurrent abscesses, malnutrition, sustained tachycardia — plus , which is profound, global, and classically reverses within 1–2 weeks of source control. This was the reversible hit; reversing it is what brought her back.

What it means

1The baseline changes the diagnosis

LVEF <50% before anthracycline is high risk and calls for cardiology referral, cardioprotection, and imaging before, during, and after therapy — not a 17-month gap. The result was in the chart; it never reached the treatment decisions.

Go deeper: a systems failure, not a person's

No single clinician erred. Oncology reacted to the echo it saw; the regimen change to R-CEOP preserved curative intent (R-CEOP retains curative potential when anthracyclines are contraindicated). What failed was the transfer: a high-risk baseline never generated the referral, the cardioprotection, or the surveillance the guideline attaches to it. The hospitalist admitting a cancer survivor with new heart failure is the person best positioned to go find that baseline rather than inherit the narrative — the admitting echo here was read as "chronic cardiomyopathy worsened by cardiotoxic chemo agents," a story the baseline contradicts.

2The "decline" after cycle 1 was noise

MUGA 43% vs echo 45% is a 2-point difference across two different modalities, inside 2D echo's own ±11-point test–retest variability. Dysfunction was recognized, not caused.

Go deeper: why the two numbers can't be compared

They measure different things. MUGA counts photons from labelled blood in the cavity — geometry-independent; 2D echo traces endocardial borders on two planes and assumes a geometric model. Across a population they correlate; in one patient they can differ by 5–10 points systematically.

The discipline cuts both ways. Her month-22 biplane LVEF of 38% vs the MUGA 43.1% does not mean she "fell short of baseline" — that claim would be exactly as unjustified as the original "decline." What can be said: she returned to her pre-treatment range, cavity size normalized, the severe MR resolved, and GLS improved from −3% to −13% (normal ≲ −18%) — the strain number is the best single marker that a substrate persists.

Practice point: serial surveillance belongs to one modality — ideally 3D echo (variability ≈6 points) with the same reader.

3The collapse was reversible; the substrate is not

Sepsis, catabolism, and tachycardia drove her below 10%. Reversing them returned her to her range — with the septal scar and an abnormal GLS still there. Treat the precipitants; keep the GDMT.

Go deeper: the evidence under each phrase

Sepsis-induced myocardial dysfunction was described in 1984: among 20 patients with septic shock studied by radionuclide angiography, half had EF <40% acutely; survivors averaged an initial EF of 32% with ventricular dilation, and EF and volumes normalized by ~10 days. It is global (distinct from takotsubo) and can be superimposed on a weak ventricle — exactly her situation.

Recovery despite stopping drugs. She improved although metoprolol and ivabradine were stopped for hypotension — pointing to reversal of precipitants, not a single agent.

Why GDMT continues anyway: in recovered dilated cardiomyopathy, withdrawing therapy led to relapse in 44% within 6 months (TRED-HF). Mid-wall fibrosis independently predicts mortality and sudden death. HF-with-improved-EF is a treatment success to be maintained, not an exit ramp.

Still actionable: iron deficiency (TSAT 12% — ferritin is inflamed and misleading; IV iron carries a Class I recommendation for symptoms), genetic testing with family screening, and re-challenge of the stopped agents as blood pressure allows.

Take-home algorithm
